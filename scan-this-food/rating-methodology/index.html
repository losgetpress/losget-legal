Scan This Food — Rating Methodology

Version 1.20 · Ruleset v1 · 10/4/2026 · Losget Press

1. Purpose and scope

Scan This Food reads the barcode of a packaged food sold in the United States, retrieves the product's record from USDA FoodData Central or, if no matching data is found there, from Open Food Facts, and rates the product against criteria based on publicly available U.S. federal nutrition information and the Nutrition Watch items the user selects. The app does not diagnose, treat, or prevent any disease, does not provide medical or dietary advice, and does not evaluate products without a barcode.

2. Data sources and display policy

The app first looks up the USDA FoodData Central Branded Foods dataset (release dated 4/30/2026). If no matching data is found in that dataset, it looks up Open Food Facts. Food data from Open Food Facts is available under the Open Database License (ODbL). Each product's data comes from one source only; data from the two sources is never combined. Product names, brands, package sizes, serving sizes, and ingredient statements are displayed exactly as recorded in the source, except that USDA FoodData Central unit codes are shown as their standard symbols (for example, GRM as g, MLT as mL, and ONZ as oz); the numbers are unchanged. Numeric values are displayed as recorded, except that values of 1 or more with more than two decimal places are rounded to two decimal places (half up); values below 1 are always displayed as recorded. Ratings use the same displayed values. The app does not otherwise round, convert, estimate, complete, or edit recorded data. Values the source does not provide are shown as "No data recognized". Values recorded per 100 g are shown separately and labeled as such. Each page that shows product data states the source and the date of the product record. When no matching data is found in either source, the app reports that no matching data was found.

3. Basis of the rating criteria

The products rated are U.S.-labeled packaged foods, so the criteria are based on U.S. federal sources:

(a) FDA consumer guidance on the Nutrition Facts label, which treats 5% of the Daily Value or less per serving as low and 20% or more as high, and which describes 400 calories or more per serving of an individual packaged food as high;

(b) FDA nutrient content claim definitions in 21 CFR Part 101 (sections 101.54, 101.60, 101.61, and 101.62);

(c) the Daily Values used on the Nutrition Facts label (21 CFR 101.9);

(d) the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030 (U.S. Department of Agriculture and U.S. Department of Health and Human Services).

Losget Press selected these criteria from the sources above and applies them as described in this document. The criteria are not issued, reviewed, or endorsed by any government agency. Where an FDA definition of "low" exists, it sets the low threshold. Where none exists, 5% of the Daily Value is used. The high threshold is 20% of the Daily Value or an FDA-stated high level. Amounts between the two are rated moderate.

4. Thresholds (per serving)

Calories: low at 40 kcal or less (21 CFR 101.60, "low calorie"); high at 400 kcal or more (FDA Nutrition Facts guidance).

Saturated fat: low at 1 g or less (21 CFR 101.62, gram criterion of "low saturated fat"); high at 4 g or more (20% of the 20 g Daily Value).

Sodium: low at 140 mg or less (21 CFR 101.61, "low sodium"); high at 460 mg or more (20% of the 2,300 mg Daily Value).

Added sugars: low at 2.5 g or less (5% of the 50 g Daily Value; no FDA "low sugar" definition exists); high at 10 g or more (20% of the Daily Value, and the per-meal maximum in the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030).

Cholesterol: low at 20 mg or less (21 CFR 101.62, milligram criterion of "low cholesterol"); high at 60 mg or more (20% of the 300 mg Daily Value).

Trans fat: low when recorded as 0 g; high at 0.5 g or more (amounts under 0.5 g may be declared as 0 g under 21 CFR 101.9; no Daily Value exists).

Total carbohydrate: low at 13.75 g or less; high at 55 g or more (5% and 20% of the 275 g Daily Value).

Dietary fiber: high at 5.6 g or more; good source at 2.8 g or more (21 CFR 101.54, 20% and 10% of the 28 g Daily Value).

Protein: high at 10 g or more; good source at 5 g or more (21 CFR 101.54, 20% and 10% of the 50 g Daily Value, applied to the recorded grams).

Kids' Nutrition (under 10): any added sugars are rated high, following the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030 advice that children avoid added sugars.

5. Nutrition Watch

Nutrition Watch item names describe nutrients, not health conditions. Each Nutrition Watch item uses a fixed set of the thresholds above and is rated with the least favorable result among its nutrients:

General Health: calories, saturated fat, sodium, added sugars.

Carbs & Added Sugars: added sugars, total carbohydrate.

Low Sodium: sodium.

Calories & Added Sugars: calories, added sugars.

Fats & Sodium: saturated fat, trans fat, sodium, cholesterol.

Low Sugar: added sugars.

High Protein: protein.

High Fiber: dietary fiber.

Low Saturated Fat: saturated fat.

Cholesterol & Fats: cholesterol, saturated fat, trans fat.

Kids' Nutrition (under 10): added sugars, sodium, saturated fat.

If no one is selected, no allergen or sulfites check is made and no Nutrition Watch selections are applied.

6. Missing data and per-100 g values

When a nutrient's amount per serving is not recorded, but its amount per 100 g and the serving size in grams are recorded, the app calculates the amount per serving for rating purposes only. The calculated amount is not displayed; the page shows the recorded per-100 g value and states that the rating is based on per-100 g values and serving size. No such calculation is made when the serving size or the per-100 g basis is a volume. If a nutrient is still missing, the app determines whether any possible value of that nutrient could change the result. If it could not, the result is shown with a note that some nutrition data was not found in the USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts record (for a label scan, that some label data is missing). If it could, the result is "Not enough data". When a nutrient is missing, the recorded amount of a nutrient that contains it is used only as an upper bound, because a part cannot exceed the whole: total fat for saturated fat and trans fat; total carbohydrate for dietary fiber, total sugars, and added sugars; and total sugars for added sugars.

Data plausibility. Before rating, Scan This Food checks each USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts record for values that are not physically possible: a serving size below the minimum plausible weight, or a nutrient amount larger than the serving itself (or larger than 100 g per 100 g). A record that fails this check is not used for rating. Scan This Food does not correct or estimate any recorded value.

Label scan. When no matching data is found in USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts, or its record does not have enough data, you can scan the package label with the camera. Scan This Food reads the Nutrition Facts panel and, when a selected person has allergens, the ingredients list. Scan This Food reads the English Nutrition Facts panel and the English ingredients list on the package (food sold in the United States must be labeled in English); a version in another language is used only to prompt you to scan the English version. Values read from the label are shown with the source "your label scan". A serving size read by the label scan is shown with its spacing tidied: a space between a number and the letters that follow it, and before an opening parenthesis (for example, "1Tbsp(15g)" is shown as "1 Tbsp (15 g)"); its letters, digits and words are kept as read. A value is used when the same reading is obtained in at least two frames. A reading obtained in only one frame is not used, and it does not count as a differing reading. Serving size readings with the same weight, where the household measure of one of them was not read or is not a recognized unit, are treated as the same serving size, not as differing readings. When two or more readings are each obtained in at least two frames, the most frequent is used. As soon as every value has been read, the values are taken at once, without waiting. The whole panel has been scanned when its title and each of the five rows that every Nutrition Facts panel must list have been read in at least two frames: Calories, Total Fat, Sodium, Total Carbohydrate, and Protein (21 CFR 101.9(c) sets the order of the rows, with Protein last; 101.9(f) keeps these five rows in the simplified format). No other line, such as the footnote or the start of the ingredients list, is used to decide that the panel has been scanned. When the whole panel has been scanned, the values are taken as soon as no value is still being read (read, but not yet the same in two frames); a value never seen is shown as having no valid data. Readings that are not physically possible are not used for rating. Readings whose combination with related numbers on the label, such as the %Daily Value, may not be plausible are used as read and are shown with a note that, if there is any discrepancy, the original package prevails. The allergen check uses the ingredients text only when the whole list has been read; otherwise the check is shown as not completed. One exception gives an earlier result: when, for every selected person who has allergens, one of those allergens has been read the same in at least two frames in the ingredients list or its "Contains" statement (not in a "may contain" or similar precautionary statement, and not in a negated form such as "peanut-free"), the ingredients list does not have to be read in full. If this happens while the Nutrition Facts panel is being read, the allergen is shown at once at the top of the camera view and the panel continues to be read; when the panel has been read, the result is given without reading the ingredients. If it happens while the ingredients are being read, the result is given at once. That allergen is reported as contained; any other allergen of that person is reported as not determined, because the list was not read in full. An allergen is reported as not listed only when the whole list has been read. When anyone selected has allergens, reading the ingredients is offered with every scan result: after the Nutrition Facts panel, when no panel is found, and for a USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts record ("Read the package ingredients"). For a USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts record, the allergen check then uses the package's ingredients, and the result states the two sources separately: ingredient check results from the package ingredients, nutrition values from that record's source. The ingredients read are not added to the USDA FoodData Central or Open Food Facts record. Scan This Food does not correct or estimate any value read from the label. Images are processed on your device and are not saved.

7. Allergen check

Only the allergens that U.S. law requires to be declared on the label are checked: the nine major food allergens of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, section 201(qq) (milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, soybeans, and sesame). Each person selects allergens from this fixed list; no other words can be added. Crustacean shellfish and tree nuts have sub-items, following FDA's 1/2025 guidance, "Questions and Answers Regarding Food Allergens, Including the Food Allergen Labeling Requirements of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (Edition 5)": shrimp, crab, lobster, and crayfish; and the tree nuts of its Table 1 (almond, Brazil nut, cashew, hazelnut, macadamia nut, pecan, pine nut, pistachio, and walnut, which includes English, Persian, black, California, and heartnut or Japanese walnuts). Selecting a category includes all of its sub-items; a sub-item can also be selected on its own. Wheat includes spelt, khorasan, einkorn, emmer, durum, and triticale.

The app searches the recorded English ingredient text, or an English ingredient list read in full from the package label, for fixed word lists and precautionary phrases such as "may contain". Exclusion words are checked first; for example, "buckwheat" is not wheat, and "flour" alone is not counted as wheat. It quotes the text that triggered each alert, exactly as recorded or read. The exemption for highly refined oils is not applied, because the app cannot tell a highly refined oil from another oil: "soybean oil" and "peanut oil" are reported like any other mention.

When a person selected only some sub-items of a category and the text names the category only by a general term, such as "tree nuts" or "shellfish", the result states that the ingredients list only the general term, not the kind, and that it may include the selected sub-items; Barcode Quick Pick then reports "Not sure". For a person who selected the whole category, a general term is reported like any other mention of that category.

Each person has one "Also avoid 'may contain'" choice, which applies to all of that person's allergens and to sulfites (section 8); it is on by default. A mention in a negated form, such as "egg-free", is shown as a caution with the quoted text, not as "contains"; any other occurrence of the same allergen word takes precedence.

If no ingredient data is recognized, the result states that the allergen check could not be done. An allergen that is not found in the ingredient text is reported as not listed, never as absent. For anyone with allergens set, Barcode Quick Pick never reports that a product fits; the result is "Doesn't fit" or "Not sure".

Allergen basis (United States only): the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, section 201(qq), as amended by the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act of 2004 and the FASTER Act of 2021 (sesame from 1/1/2023); FDA, "Questions and Answers Regarding Food Allergens, Including the Food Allergen Labeling Requirements of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (Edition 5)", 1/2025; and, for sulfites, 21 CFR 101.100(a)(4). The food-labeling rules of other countries may name allergens that this app does not check.

8. Sulfites

Sulfites are not an allergen and are never presented as one. Each person can turn on Sulfites as a separate setting. Under 21 CFR 101.100(a)(4), a sulfiting agent (sulfur dioxide, sodium sulfite, sodium bisulfite, potassium bisulfite, sodium metabisulfite, or potassium metabisulfite) present in the finished food at 10 parts per million or more, or with a technical effect in the food, must be declared on the label. The app cannot measure amounts; it relies on that labeling rule. It searches the same ingredient text as the allergen check for the names of these sulfiting agents (21 CFR 182.3616, 182.3637, 182.3739, 182.3766, 182.3798, and 182.3862) and for the words "sulfite" and "sulfiting agent", in U.S. and British spelling. When sulfites are listed, the result shows "Contains sulfites" with the quoted text, as a warning. A precautionary statement naming sulfites is shown as "May contain sulfites" with the quoted text, and follows the person's "Also avoid 'may contain'" choice. When sulfites are not found, nothing is shown about them. If no ingredient data is recognized, the result states that it cannot be determined whether the product contains sulfites. In a label scan, a person with Sulfites set is treated as a person with allergens (section 6): the ingredients list is read, sulfites read for certain are shown at once at the top of the camera view, and they count toward the earlier result. For anyone with Sulfites set, Barcode Quick Pick never reports that a product fits; when sulfites are found, the result is "Doesn't fit".

9. Barcode Quick Pick

DOESN'T FIT: an allergen or sulfites are found, or any selected Nutrition Watch item is rated red (Limit, or Below good source for High Protein and High Fiber).

NOT SURE: allergens or Sulfites are set but not found, a negated allergen mention or a general term of a category is found, or a result cannot be determined.

FITS: all selected Nutrition Watch items are rated low or moderate and no allergens or Sulfites are set.

10. Consistency and independence

The same rules apply to every product and every brand. Losget Press does not accept payment or any other consideration to influence a rating.

11. Limitations

Ratings consider only the recorded amounts of the nutrients listed above. They do not assess overall nutritional quality, degree of processing, or ingredient quality. FDA claim definitions are stated per Reference Amount Customarily Consumed (RACC); this app applies them to the serving size recorded in the source, which may differ. The FDA definitions of "low saturated fat" and "low cholesterol" include additional conditions that this app does not apply. Ratings are general screening information for a 2,000-calorie reference diet and do not account for individual health needs. Losget Press is not affiliated with or endorsed by USDA, FDA, HHS, or Open Food Facts.
